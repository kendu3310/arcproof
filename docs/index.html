What this is

Paying for something online is a solved problem on Arc. Knowing you got what you paid for is not.

Circle's x402 SDK settles payments on Arc, and does it well. But search its entire public API for receipt, attest, proof or deliverable and you find nothing. A buyer can prove it paid. It cannot prove what it received.

That gap matters once the buyer is software. A person can open a 3D model and see it was mangled. An autonomous agent gets bytes back, and if they turn out to be bad, nothing ties them to the provider: it can say it sent something else, and nobody can show otherwise.

Aernyth closes that gap and nothing else. When a service returns a result, it signs the digests of the exact bytes in and out, and that signature reaches a contract on Arc mainnet within seconds. The buyer hashes its own copies and compares. From then on the provider cannot claim it sent something else.

That is not the same as proving the result is good. The provider writes the receipt, so a provider that returns a broken file and records the broken file's digest passes every check above. Quality is a separate check, run by the buyer against the content itself — this page recounts the triangles of a model, or measures an image, rather than taking the service's word. A check like that is what lets software reject bad output; the receipt is what makes the rejection stick to the provider who caused it.

It composes with Circle's middleware rather than replacing it — gateway.require() takes the money, withReceipt() records what was handed over. Compression ratios shown here are a property of the asset, not of the service; your own model may compress far less.
